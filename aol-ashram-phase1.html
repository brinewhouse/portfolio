This is where Permaville started

Immediately after Phase I finished, Brian taught a 12-day Permaville training course on-site — students from the Ashram and several other countries learned the full design-to-installation-to-financing methodology. The Phase II proposal in this same document was designed by that class. Brian is credited in the project manual as author of Permaville: How to Design a Permanent Village.

The Site & the Brief

A 7-acre triangular parcel behind the Ashram's Goushala — sloped, rocky, mostly bare vegetation, with an existing pond at the top and bottom of the property. Venkatesh Dharmraj, a Bangalore resident affiliated with the Ashram, had visited the Panya Project in Thailand earlier in 2013 and arranged for Panya's consultants to design and install a demonstration site. Phase I addressed the highest-priority needs first: water, food, shelter.

Design: Brecht Deriemaker and Nick Tittle. Installation: Troy Johnson and Brian Newhouse. All labor was manual — no power tools, one backhoe pass for the swales.

Mission & Vision (as written, 2013)

Mission: build a model permaculture site and education program at the Ashram.

Vision: set up satellite projects in villages around India and other Ashram centers; grow organic food for the Ashram; become a leading institute for permaculture education in India.
