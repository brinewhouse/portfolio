Humanure Compost Toilets

Four chambers (2×3×2m each), replacing a prior structure whose wood floorboards had rotted. Concrete footers, brick walls, steel-beam-supported floor panels (redesigned mid-build after the first panels broke), bamboo-and-cob walls. Two stalls opened for use, nicknamed "The Shateau"; two more held in reserve to rotate in as chambers fill.

Materials + labor: 20,540 ฿ (~$660 USD, 2013 rates)

Pond Liner Installation

Four donated 1,000kg rolls (40m × 4.85m) of Bentonix BFG 5000 bentonite clay liner had sat in Panya's garage for years. Rather than crane them out, the crew built hand pulleys — four pulleys per roll cut the effective pull weight from 1,000kg to 250kg — and moved everything with the Panya truck and 10-12 people at a time.

Liner installed
3,500 kg (of 4,000 kg donated)
Overlap between liner pieces
30 cm, to prevent leakage
Trench profile
1m flat shelf + 30×30cm trench, backhoe-dug
Cash cost
~$600 USD (20,000 ฿) — 4 days excavator rental, the only paid cost

Post-fill plan: surround with a food forest, plant water plants against evaporation, breed fish/frogs/birds/bees, add storage tanks for the dry season.

Multi-Barrier Water Filter

A 5-stage gravity filter — settling tank, aerator, then gravel/sand/charcoal barrels in sequence — modeled from a design at the Pun Pun Self-Reliance Center, engineered by Aqueous Solutions (Josh Kearns and Nate Reents), sketched under the Permaville Education and Consultancy Group banner.

Output
1 L/minute — 1,440 L/day
Bio-charcoal
Burned on-site from eucalyptus
Maintenance
Bleed valve at 1 year if flow slows; test/replace charcoal at 5 years

Materials: ~20,500 ฿ (~$680 USD, 2013 rates) — cement rings, cement, aggregate, bio-charcoal, PVC piping, sheet metal tower caps

Greywater System

Zero-cost design using the same bentonite liner approach — 1m shelf + 30cm trench, routed to feed a new plant/fish/bird ecosystem. Fully documented; construction didn't reach completion before Brian's departure.
